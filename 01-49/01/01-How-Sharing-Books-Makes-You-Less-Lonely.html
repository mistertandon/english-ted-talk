Chapter I

A library of 180 owners

Short pause A few months ago, 180 passionate individuals came together in Singapore to build something rather extraordinary: a community library.

In a city fortunate enough to already have a world-class, highly innovative, and much-beloved public library system — of which I am a proud beneficiary — you might well ask, what makes our library truly distinct?Beat

Long pause Show slide This is the library we built.

We are nestled in the heartlands of Singapore, short pause off the beaten track, between a 50-year-old furniture shop and an antique store, surrounded by birds, trees, and — believe it or not — quite a few chickens. Laughter - pause

What we have created is what we call a shared library. There are 180 shelves, each one rented, curated and maintained by a different person.Emphasis

For just 45 Singapore dollars a month, you become a bookshelf owner. Short pause And being a bookshelf owner is an absolute delight. You get to share your favourite books, you curate your own tiny corner, and you refresh your collection whenever you fancy.

Not only do we house books, but we also house stories, identities, and memories. If you curate a shelf with genuine passion, it invariably reflects your personality.

Every shelf in our library is like a little pocket universe — an intriguing, almost voyeuristic glimpse into someone’s heart and mind.

If you wander through our aisles, you quite often find yourself pausing in front of a shelf thinking, "I think I know this person," even though you have never met them in your life. If you visit us tomorrow, you will discover 180 entirely unique worlds.Emphasis

Long pause Another defining feature is that we are entirely volunteer-run. A dedicated roster of about 40 incredibly kind volunteer librarians takes turns to keep our doors open six days a week, twelve hours a day.

Some are bookshop owners with their own shelf. Others are simply neighbours who wanted to be part of something meaningful — doctors, former lawyers, students, mothers, freelancers, retirees. We even have a five-year-old librarian amongst us. We are all birds of a feather, and what binds us is simple.Idiom

We love books, we believe in the transformative power of books, and we absolutely relish being surrounded by them.
Emphasis

So, whenever we are open, anyone is welcome. Come in, sit down, read a book for free, make a genuine connection, enjoy a cup of coffee freshly brewed by a fellow reader, and just let the stress of modern life melt off your shoulders. If we had unlimited space, we would house thousands more narratives and welcome the whole neighbourhood.

Chapter II

The spark in Japan

Now, how did this labour of love come about?

In April this year, I took a sabbatical from my work as a photographer and travelled to Japan, where I stumbled upon a small, participatory micro-library with a remarkably similar concept. Short pause I was utterly captivated. There was something about that space that truly enticed me.

Standing in that little library in Japan, even though I could not read a single Japanese title, I sensed a profoundly powerful energy of love and care. If we hadn’t taken that leap of faith back in April, this grassroots initiative would never have materialised.Emphasis

It was then I thought it would be wonderful to replicate something like this in Singapore. So I turned to social media to rally the troops.

If we weren’t so utterly committed to community stewardship, we wouldn’t have secured this wonderful hyperlocal space earlier.

If I hadn’t stumbled upon that Japanese micro-library, we wouldn’t be standing here today celebrating our bibliophilic ecosystem.

Long pause - Slow delivery Were we to expand this shared library model to every heartland estate, we might just redefine Singapore’s reading culture altogether. At the end of the day, it has become a real home away from home.Idiom

Chapter III

One Instagram post

Short pause So I returned to Singapore and decided to put my vision out there. I drafted a simple Instagram post outlining what a small, shared library in Singapore could look like.

My thinking was quite straightforward. I thought if no one responded, I would simply forget about the whole idea. But if I could get 50 people on board, I would be delighted to give this rather impractical, dreamy, and idealistic notion a try.Beat

Short pause I finished writing, pressed post, and went straight back to work.

What happened next was quite beyond my wildest dreams. Not only did people respond, but by the end of the very first day, I had received 300 direct messages from people genuinely willing to put money down to rent a shelf and build a library together. If you come by on a Tuesday, you will meet Raymond and truly feel that same passion first-hand.

That night, I was incredibly moved. I wore my heart on my sleeve reading those messages — I could feel everyone’s sheer fire and passion. If people share a common vision, a community flourishes.

Living in Singapore, where life is so practical and pragmatic, this felt like a moment of collective jailbreak. It was as if many people had just been waiting for the right moment to do something meaningful together. What struck me most was the realisation that so many people still care deeply about books.Idiom

Long pause
So I decided to take the plunge. Idiom - Emphasis: take the plunge

Three days after the post, I found a space to rent. On the fourth day, I signed the lease. I am an Aquarius, so I tend to follow my intuition. Laughter - pause

If I hadn’t posted that vision on Instagram, none of this collective endeavour would have ever materialised.

With the help of many people — most of them complete strangers at the time — we built our grassroots library in just three months. Show slide - Emphasis: literally building This is a picture of us literally building it — installing the flooring together.

Chapter IV

Meet the shelves

Short pause So who are these 180 wonderful individuals who decided to do this with me?

Raymond · CDs & Japan

This is Raymond’s shelf. Raymond is passionate about collecting CDs and books about Japan — those are the two treasures you will find on his shelf. Every Tuesday, he sets up a little stall outside our library where he sells his second-hand collection. If I had more time, I would curate a shelf entirely on analogue culture just like his. He also brews pay-as-you-wish coffee for anyone who drops by.

Wanqi & Liam · mother and son

This is Wanqi and Liam — mother and son. Liam is the five-year-old librarian I mentioned earlier. Short pause So far, we have been thoroughly impressed by his work ethic. Their shelf is split equally — half features Liam’s favourite children’s books, fostering wonderful intergenerational literacy, and half showcases Wanqi’s collection. Liam decorated it himself. It looks absolutely brilliant.Laughter

The Kin San shelf · Hong Kong

And this particular bookshelf owner decided to preserve a piece of history. She has shared books that once sat on the shelves of Kin San, a much-loved Hong Kong bookshop that has sadly closed down. She has even replicated a miniature version of the banner that used to hang outside Kin San, which reads, “ideas are bulletproof.”

In a way, Kin San lives on right here inside our library.
Emphasis - Slow delivery
Mira · letters & a mailbox

This is Mira. Mira is a preschool teacher who champions cultural preservation. She firmly believes in the power of letter writing, especially for children, which is why you will find a little mailbox on her shelf. So, apart from enjoying the amazing selection of books she has curated, you can also write her a letter. Mixed Conditional - E

If I weren’t an Aquarius who follows her intuition, I wouldn’t have signed that lease on day four.

If we hadn’t built this together as strangers, we wouldn’t be a family of bibliophiles today.

Chapter V

Why it works

One of the most frequent questions we are asked is how this community-owned library stays open. On the face of it, it does not make economic sense.Short pauseBeat

The simple answer is this: 180 people decided to pull together to build a grassroots book space for the local community. Emphasis: pull together

What is truly remarkable is that this collective ownership model is actually highly practical. If you share resources freely, trust grows naturally. We share the rent between 180 people, so no single person bears the financial burden of keeping this space open.

The other question we are often asked is, what do we gain from doing this? Well, we certainly do not get paid in the traditional financial sense.

But we have been open for about four months now, and for many of us, this library has become a rare home from home. On most days, our library also doubles as a community centre — a genuine third place. Apart from the usual book clubs and women’s sharing circles, people have hosted curry nights and hot-pot nights for 40 people. Short pauseIdiom

If you visit our midnight library, which we run twice a month, you will feel that sense of belonging immediately. We remain open until late — or at least until the last person wishes to stay.

Chapter VI

Community in every corner

I see community in every corner of our little library.

I see it in the people who bring food, drinks, ideas, and resources to freely share with one another. If we charged late fees, people might not rush to return books so eagerly. Yet they do. I believe it is because they can sense the love and care poured into every shelf, and because they genuinely want others to have access to the books.

If 180 people hadn’t decided to take the plunge, we wouldn’t have this home today.

I see community in the little children who come in. They remind each other to take care of the books, not to bend the pages, and to put each book back in its rightful place.

I see community in our volunteer librarians, who pour their heart and soul into cleaning the library, cataloguing books, and ensuring the library remains a welcoming social infrastructure for everyone. Idiom - Emphasis: heart and soul If we weren’t so deeply rooted in volunteer stewardship, we wouldn’t have built such strong social capital so quickly.

Long pause - Slow delivery And because of all this, Singapore feels a little different to me these days. It feels a little more generous, warm, open, kind, joyful, and trusting.

In a world where people feel increasingly lonely and society feels increasingly fragmented, this little library has given us — or at least it has given me — a feeling of power. If we had relied on a traditional commercial model, this space would have closed within months.

Not the kind of power that is heavy and destructive, but the kind of power that is light and regenerative.

Long pause - Emphasis Were we to replicate this commons-based model across Singapore, we might fundamentally reshape the city’s social fabric.

This little library has given me renewed hope that we do have the power to come together to build what we believe is missing in our lives, in our city, in our world.

And sometimes, that missing thing is a small, shared, community-owned library — a true labour of love.
